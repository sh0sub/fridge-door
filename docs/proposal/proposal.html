Fridge Door

Uliya Skill for UGREEN AI NAS · Round 1 proposal · Track: Bring Memories to Life

1. What does your Skill do?

Fridge Door reads the photos parents take of school notices and children's drawings, already backed up to the NAS, and turns them each week into a dated to-do list and a growing album per child.

2. Who is your target user, and what is their problem?

Parents of kindergarten and primary-school children. Reply slips and fee notices come home on paper, get photographed, sink into the camera roll, and the deadline passes. Drawings pile up with no child or date attached. It is harder still for parents who cannot read the school's language.

3. What is your solution?

Each week the parent says "sort out this week's school papers". The Skill finds new photos in the phone-backup folder, reads them with the NAS's OCR and vision model, and separates notices, drawings and the rest. After one preview it saves a checklist digest and copies drawings into each child's keepsake folder.

4. What is the main user flow?

Ask; confirm folder, children and dates; photos are read; preview; correct in plain words ("IMG_0107 is Jun's") and add what the child said; approve; report starting with the next deadline.

5. How is your idea different from existing tools or common AI solutions?

Unlike calendar apps and chat assistants that read one uploaded photo, Fridge Door remembers: tags mark handled photos, unticked deadlines carry into the next digest, and each child's album follows its folder, so new drawings appear by themselves. It works on photos your phone already backs up, with nothing to pick or upload. It also keeps what the child said about each drawing and collects it into an art journal.

6. Which UGREEN AI NAS features does your Skill use, and at which step?

Steps 1–3: ug-filesystem reads the last digest and lists new photos; ug-filemgr reads the done tag. Steps 4–5: ug-photo OCR and visual check. Step 9: file-workflow batch copy. Step 10: ug-photo folder-following album per child. Step 11: ug-knowledge saves the digest. Step 12: ug-filemgr tags. On request: ug-document builds the art journal.

7. What is your technical approach?

A Markdown Skill that only delegates to four registered experts, passing on only real results. Inputs: folder, children's names, language, period. Each write waits for the parent's yes, then the NAS approval prompt. The Skill uploads nothing: the NAS's photo expert reads photos in place, and Uliya writes summaries with the owner's configured model.

8. What have you built so far? What is not built yet? What still needs testing on a NAS?

Built: the package for kit 0.7.7. Our local port of the checker rules reports no errors; 214 automated tests replay the workflow against simulated expert replies. Not built: handling tuned to real expert replies. Not yet run on a NAS: Korean and English OCR, the folder-following album, re-reading the digest, the art journal.

9. How will you test your Skill if you become a Finalist?

Test kit: 16 invented photos. Core 1, first batch: eight notices give six deadlines and two events; two signed drawings go to two children's folders and albums. Core 2, "continue": three more drawings join the existing albums and open deadlines carry over. Edge: a refused approval ends as "Partly done".

10. What are the known limits, risks, or outside dependencies?

Images only, twelve per batch. Handwriting and poor light lower OCR quality; unreadable photos are listed, never guessed. Expert reply formats are assumed; the test plan lists nineteen assumptions and a fallback table. Depends on built-in NAS experts, photo indexing and the owner's model.

Entrant: individual. Third-party components: none. External services and data flows: the Skill calls none; text written by Uliya is processed by the model the NAS owner configured. AI tools: Claude Code (Anthropic) helped write and review the Skill files, the checker port, the tests and this proposal; UGOS Skill Creation Tool (uliya-skill-kit 0.7.7) was the reference. Source and tests: github.com/sh0sub/fridge-door (the checker there is our own Python port of the kit's check rules and is not part of the Skill package)
